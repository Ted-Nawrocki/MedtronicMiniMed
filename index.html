Strategic Context

A separation eighteen months in the making

The exchange offer that opened on September 14, 2026 is the final step in a sequence that began in May 2025, when Medtronic announced its plan to separate its Diabetes business within an 18-month time frame.1

MiniMed Group, Inc. was incorporated in Delaware on February 27, 2025. Medtronic's Diabetes business was transferred to MiniMed in the Separation, which was completed together with MiniMed's IPO on March 9, 2026.2 MiniMed sold 28,000,000 shares at $20.00 per share, and its stock began trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol MMED on March 6, 2026. Net proceeds to MiniMed were approximately $538 million, and at the closing Medtronic owned approximately 90.03% of MiniMed common stock.3 As of September 3, 2026, Medtronic beneficially owned 252,813,348 of the 281,349,931 MiniMed shares outstanding, approximately 89.86%.2

The S-4 describes two ways to divest the remaining stake: a spin-off, in which Medtronic would make a pro rata distribution of MiniMed stock to all Medtronic shareholders, or a split-off, in which Medtronic exchanges Medtronic shares for MiniMed stock, or a combination of the two.2 Medtronic chose the split-off. Only holders who want MiniMed tender their Medtronic shares for it.

The 225,361,295 MMED shares offered represent approximately 80.1% of MiniMed's outstanding common stock as of September 3, 2026. If the offer is completed but less than 80.1% is exchanged because it is not fully subscribed, Medtronic intends to effect a spin-off on the same day. The offer is also conditioned on an opinion from Skadden that the exchange will qualify as tax-free under Section 355 of the Internal Revenue Code.2

The Exchange Offer also presents an opportunity for Medtronic to acquire a high number of outstanding Medtronic Ordinary Shares at one time, and in one transaction, without reducing Medtronic's cash.

Transaction Anatomy

Three steps, one separation

The IPO, the exchange offer, and the backstop are not independent events. Each one makes the next possible.

01
Equity Capital Markets

The MiniMed IPO

Priced March 5, 2026 at $20.00 per share and closed March 9. The IPO also established a public market for MMED on Nasdaq, and the exchange ratio formula is based on the Nasdaq trading prices of MMED.4

02
Voluntary Corporate Action

The Exchange Offer

Launched September 14, 2026. Medtronic offers up to 225,361,295 MMED shares at a 7% discount, capped at 4.5939 MMED shares per MDT share. Accepted MDT shares are redeemed and cancelled. Expires 12:00 midnight New York City time at the end of October 9, unless extended.5

03
Mandatory Backstop

Spin-Off or Clean-Up

If the offer is completed but less than 80.1% of MMED is exchanged, Medtronic intends to effect a spin-off on the same day. Any stake that remains after that would be divested later through a spin-off, split-off, debt-for-equity exchange, or a combination.2

Corporate Finance Note

In a split-off, the parent exchanges shares of the subsidiary for its own shares. Here, every Medtronic share accepted in the offer is legally acquired as a redemption under Article 12 of Medtronic's articles of association and then cancelled. The same transaction that separates MiniMed therefore also reduces the number of Medtronic shares outstanding.2

Exchange Ratio Mechanics

How the exchange ratio is set

The ratio is fixed from prices during the Averaging Period of October 5, 6 and 7, then locked two trading days before expiration. Price moves on October 8 and 9 do not change it.2

Final exchange ratio = the lesser of Average MDT Price ÷ ( 93% × Average MMED Price ) and 4.5939

Each average is the simple arithmetic mean of the daily volume-weighted average prices on the NYSE (Bloomberg page MDT UN) and Nasdaq (MMED UW) from 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. New York City time, counting trade adjustments reported by 4:10 p.m. There is no lower limit. The upper limit reflects a 12% discount to MMED based on September 11, 2026 closing prices of $90.96 for MDT and $22.50 for MMED; $90.96 divided by 88% of $22.50 equals 4.5939.2

Structural Analysis

Why a split-off, not a spin-off

Both structures can separate a subsidiary tax-free under Section 355. They differ in who ends up owning the new stock, and in what happens to the parent's share count.

The index fund problem

Author's analysis of general market practice. The offer documents do not discuss index funds.

In a spin-off, every holder of the parent receives the new shares whether they want them or not. That includes index funds, ETFs, and mutual funds that own Medtronic because of its index membership, sector, size, or dividend. A fund tracking an index that includes Medtronic has no mandate to hold MiniMed unless MiniMed belongs to the same index.

Holders in that position commonly sell the new shares soon after distribution. That selling, known as flowback, lands on the new company just as it is trying to establish its own shareholder base.

A split-off reverses the default. Passive funds that need to stay aligned with a benchmark generally have little reason to tender, so MiniMed shares go to investors who choose them. Medtronic states that the Divestment will create a MiniMed stockholder base aligned with MiniMed's business and financial profile. MiniMed does not expect to pay dividends for the foreseeable future, so income-focused Medtronic holders can stay where they are.2

A buyback paid in stock

Medtronic describes the exchange as a way to reduce its outstanding shares in one transaction without reducing its cash.2 At the September 11 indicative ratio, the base offer would retire:

~51.7MMDT shares retired
~$4.73BValue at the indicative Average MDT Price

The first two figures are the author's calculation from prospectus inputs: 225,361,295 divided by the 4.3599 indicative ratio, then multiplied by the $91.5244 indicative Average MDT Price used to derive that ratio. The 49,056,639 share figure at the upper limit is stated in the prospectus. The cost to Medtronic is the discount: when the upper limit is not in effect, tendering holders receive approximately $107.53 of MMED for each $100 of MDT accepted, measured at Averaging Period prices.

    The Corporate Actions Perspective

    What custody and operations teams should track

    Deadlines below are taken from the prospectus and Medtronic's September 17 correction. The prospectus directs beneficial owners to confirm timing with the institution that holds their shares, which may set earlier deadlines.

    Expiration and withdrawal

    Tenders and withdrawals are permitted until 12:00 midnight New York City time at the end of October 9. DTC is expected to remain open only until 5:00 p.m. After that, withdrawals of shares tendered through DTC require a written or email notice, in DTC's form, sent by the tendering institution directly to the exchange agent.2

    Guaranteed delivery correction

    The notice of guaranteed delivery must reach the exchange agent by 12:00 midnight at the end of the expiration date. Medtronic's September 17 8-K corrected a typographical error: the shares, letter of transmittal or agent's message, and other documents must then arrive by 5:00 p.m. New York City time on the second NYSE trading day after the notice is executed, not by midnight as originally printed.6

    Final ratio announcement

    By 9:00 a.m. on October 8, with Medtronic aiming to announce on the evening of October 7. Indicative ratios are posted daily on the information agent's website from the third trading day of the offer.2

    Proration announcement

    The prospectus is inconsistent. Q&A Item 20 says the preliminary proration factor comes by 9:00 a.m. on the business day after expiration; the Summary and "The Exchange Offer" section say the trading day. The S-4 defines a business day as excluding U.S. federal holidays. Monday, October 12, 2026 is a federal holiday (Columbus Day), and it is not an NYSE holiday, so the two readings point to October 12 and October 13.7,8 Confirm the date with the information agent. Medtronic also says it will disclose whether it is accepting the additional De Minimis Increase Amount together with the preliminary proration report.1,2

    Odd lots and fractional shares

    Beneficial holders of fewer than 100 MDT shares who tender all of them are not prorated. No fractional MMED shares are issued; the exchange agent aggregates and sells them and distributes the net cash proceeds.2

    Equity awards and ESPP

    Vested options must be exercised by 4:00 p.m. at least two trading days before expiration for the shares to be tendered. Unvested options, RSUs, and PSUs cannot be tendered. ESPP shares that have met the one-year holding period may be tendered under separate plan procedures, which may have earlier deadlines.2

    Hart-Scott-Rodino

    If a tendering holder would acquire enough MMED to exceed the $133.9 million Hart-Scott-Rodino threshold and no exemption applies, Medtronic and the holder must make filings and the holder must pay the filing fee. This could delay that holder's exchange.2

    Key identifiers and agents

    Subject security: Medtronic ordinary shares, CUSIP G5960L103. Exchange agent: Equiniti Trust Company, LLC. Information agent: D.F. King & Co., Inc. Dealer managers: Goldman Sachs & Co. LLC and BofA Securities, Inc.2,9

    Corporate Actions Note: Record Date Dividend

    On August 20, 2026, Medtronic's board approved a quarterly cash dividend of $0.72 per share, payable October 16 to holders of record at the close of business on September 25. Because the record date falls before the offer closes, holders who validly tender still receive the dividend on those shares.2 Some positions will already be tendered when the record date passes, and accepted shares will be cancelled before the pay date. Make sure the dividend follows the beneficial owner on those positions, including any shares returned through proration.

    Current Status and Forward View

    Three ways the offer can end

    Medtronic is not required to complete the offer unless, among other conditions, at least 112,680,647 MMED shares will be issued in the exchange. Medtronic may waive conditions, subject to limited exceptions. Beyond that, the outcome depends on how many MDT shares are tendered.2

    In Progress / Sept 14 to Oct 9, 2026

    The offer period

    Medtronic holders decide whether to tender. Indicative ratios are published daily. The final ratio will be known by 9:00 a.m. on October 8, one full trading day before expiration.

    Scenario / Oversubscribed

    Full exit from MiniMed

    Tenders are prorated, except qualifying odd lots. Medtronic currently intends to accept additional MDT shares up to the De Minimis Increase Amount (not more than 2% of outstanding MDT shares) without extending the offer, exchanging its remaining 27,452,053 MMED shares. At the 4.5939 upper limit, that would be 5,975,762 additional MDT shares, and Medtronic would hold no MiniMed stock.2

    Scenario / Fully subscribed

    A residual stake

    If the offer is fully subscribed and Medtronic accepts no additional shares, Medtronic would own approximately 9.76% of MiniMed, based on shares outstanding as of September 3, 2026, and intends to divest it later through a spin-off, split-off, debt-for-equity exchange, or a combination.2

    Scenario / Undersubscribed

    Same-day spin-off

    If the offer is completed but less than 80.1% of MMED is exchanged because it is not fully subscribed, Medtronic intends to effect a spin-off on the same day. Holders cannot participate in that spin-off with MDT shares that were accepted in the exchange.2

    A spin-off hands MiniMed to every Medtronic holder. A split-off hands it to the ones who asked for it, and shrinks Medtronic's share count in the process.
